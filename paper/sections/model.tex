\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Physical system}

We consider a planar thermoresponsive gel slab of reference
half-thickness $H_0$ containing an immobilised catalyst that catalyses
an exothermic, irreversible, first-order reaction. The slab is
in contact at its free surface with a well-mixed bath of constant
temperature $T_\infty$ and constant reactant concentration $c_b$;
$\xi=X/H_0\in[0,1]$ is a Lagrangian coordinate, with $\xi=0$ the
symmetry plane and $\xi=1$ the free surface. The spherical and
membrane variants of the same reaction--swelling
coupling~\cite{Siegel1988,Siegel1993,Boissonade2003,BoissonadeDeKepper2011}
introduce additional radial-stretch and curvature effects that we
defer to a separate study.

\subsection{Governing equations}

The conservative variables are the local swelling ratio $J$, the
reactant content per reference volume $W=Ju$, the dimensionless
temperature excess $\theta=(T-T_\infty)/\Delta T_*$, and the
algebraic solvent chemical potential $m$.  Here
$u=W/J$ is the bath-normalized reactant concentration,
$\phi=\pp/J$ is the polymer volume fraction, and
$\Delta T_*=(-\Delta H_r)c_b/C_*$ is the adiabatic reaction
temperature rise.  The dimensionless first-principles model
(Appendix~\ref{app:nondim}) is
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:governing}
\begin{align}
  J_t + q_\xi &= 0,
  & q &= -\Ms_\mathrm{ref}(J,\theta)\,m_\xi, \label{eq:gov_J}\\[4pt]
  W_t + n_\xi &= -\Da\,J\,R_b,
  & n &= u\,q-\delta\,\Ds_\mathrm{ref}(J,\theta)\,u_\xi,
  \label{eq:gov_u}\\[4pt]
  \Cs_\mathrm{ref}(J)\,\theta_t + h_\xi &= \Da\,J\,R_b,
  & h &= -\alpha\,\Ks_\mathrm{ref}(J)\,\theta_\xi,
  \label{eq:gov_theta}\\[4pt]
  m &= m_\mathrm{mix}(J,\theta)+m_\mathrm{el}(J)-\ell^2J_{\xi\xi}.
  \label{eq:gov_m}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The subscript ``ref'' emphasizes that $\Ds_\mathrm{ref}$ and
$\Ks_\mathrm{ref}$ multiply gradients in the reference coordinate
and that $\Cs_\mathrm{ref}$ is a heat capacity per reference volume.
When current-space material coefficients are used, the optional
metric-corrected closure is
$\Ds_\mathrm{ref}=\Ds_\mathrm{cur}/J$,
$\Ks_\mathrm{ref}=\Ks_\mathrm{cur}/J$, and
$\Cs_\mathrm{ref}=J\Cs_\mathrm{cur}$.  The baseline model treats the
listed coefficients as effective reference-coordinate quantities.
The enthalpy-advection term proportional to
$\partial_\xi[(\Theta_\infty+\theta)q]$ is omitted from the baseline
and retained only as a diagnostic estimate.

The reaction rate is
\begin{equation}
  R_b = u^n\,A(\phi)\,
  \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{\Gamma_A\,\theta}{1+\varepsilon_T\,\theta}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:rxn}
\end{equation}
with a phenomenological porosity/accessibility factor
\begin{equation}
  A(\phi)=A_\mathrm{min}+(1-A_\mathrm{min})\,
  \bar p^{\,m_\mathrm{act}}, \qquad
  \bar p=\mathrm{clip}\!\left[
    \frac{1-\phi}{1-\phi_\mathrm{ref}},0,1\right].
  \label{eq:accessibility}
\end{equation}
The diffusivity barrier uses the analogous
$\Ds_\mathrm{ref}=D_0[D_\mathrm{min}+(1-D_\mathrm{min})
\bar p^{\,m_\mathrm{diff}}]$ in the baseline reference-coordinate
closure.  The solvent chemical potential is derived from the
Flory--Rehner free energy with
$\chi(\theta,\phi)=\chi_\infty+S_\chi\theta+\chi_1\phi$:
\begin{equation}
  m_\mathrm{mix}
  =\ln(1-\phi)+\phi+\chi(\theta,\phi)\phi^2
  -\phi^2(1-\phi)\,\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\phi},
  \qquad \frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\phi}=\chi_1.
  \label{eq:mmix_main}
\end{equation}
If the final correction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mmix_main} is omitted, the
parameter is interpreted only as an effective osmotic interaction
parameter, not as the derivative of a strict free energy.

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

At the symmetry plane ($\xi=0$) all fluxes vanish. At the free
surface ($\xi=1$):
\begin{align}
  q\big|_1&=\Bi_\mu\,(m-m_b), &
  n\big|_1&=\Bi_c\,(u-1), &
  h\big|_1&=\Bi_T\,\theta, &
  J_\xi\big|_1&=0,
  \label{eq:bc}
\end{align}
with $q=n=h=0$ and $J_\xi=0$ at $\xi=0$.  The reactant condition is
therefore a total-flux condition on
$n=u q-\delta\Ds_\mathrm{ref}u_\xi$, not a diffusion-only boundary
condition.  The bath chemical potential $m_b$ is retained explicitly;
one may shift to $\tilde m=m-m_b$ only if the same shifted variable is
used everywhere.
